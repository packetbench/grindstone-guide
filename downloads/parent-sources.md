# Wrestling parent orientation research and sources

Prepared for Mat Rats and Valkyries families at Grindstone Wrestling Club, a sanctioned program under the Westminster Area Recreation Council. Research checked September 25, 2026.

The updated package contains a four-page handout with a 16-question FAQ and a common-penalties page, a separate one-page FAQ, a parent code of conduct, a welcoming matside sign, an editable presentation, and a speaker script for approximately 10 minutes plus questions. Slides 1–11 are the talk, slide 12 opens questions, and slides 13–16 are optional references. The complete spoken script is also in the PowerPoint notes. The presentation is informational, with narrated examples and no quizzes, live demonstrations or parent exercises.

## Rules scope

This orientation teaches **folkstyle**. USA Wrestling's current online rule book, labeled the 2027 edition and updated September 13, 2026 when checked, uses NFHS scoring with modifications in **section 25**. Much of that same book explains freestyle and Greco-Roman, whose scoring must not be substituted into this guide. The official USA Wrestling Folkstyle Level 1 curriculum supplies the position categories and teaching demonstrations.

Exact definitions were checked in the accessible **2024–25 NFHS Wrestling Rules Book**, hosted by Utah USA Wrestling, then checked against the official **2025–26 and 2026–27 changes**. The earlier book is identified by its actual edition; it is not represented as the current full rule book. The changes checked do not replace the 3-point takedown, 1-point escape, 2-point reversal, ordinary 2/3/4 near fall, standard 2-second pin, or ordinary period-choice structure used here.

No dated, league-issued 2026–27 MMWL rule book was located publicly. Grindstone confirms its Mat Rats novice and advanced teams participate in the Mid Maryland Wrestling League. A detailed **Predators Wrestling member-club overview** corroborates regular-season pairings by age, weight and experience and describes three one-minute periods. It is not treated as governing current Grindstone instructions. The presentation qualifies that timing; the handout directs parents to the coach for current timing and overtime.

## Key facts and their evidence

| Claim taught | Evidence and precision |
|---|---|
| Mat Rats and Valkyries are Grindstone programs under WARC | Official Grindstone home and team pages. Correct spellings appear throughout. |
| The youngest group does not automatically compete | The Mat Rats page describes a first-year Pups clinic for ages 4–6 with no competitions. Its wider program is ages 4–14. Confirm each child's current group. |
| Valkyries families can use this guide at folkstyle events | Valkyries is the girls-only program for ages 5–14 and describes optional competition. Its particular event determines the rules and requirements. Girls do not automatically mean freestyle. |
| Takedown 3, escape 1, reversal 2 | NFHS Rules 5 and 9-1; the 2024–25 changes and interpretations confirm the three-point takedown. A direct reversal does not also earn an escape point. |
| Near fall ordinarily awards 2, 3 or 4 | NFHS Rule 5-11-2 and 2024–25 changes. Continuous qualifying exposure for 2/3/4 seconds earns 2/3/4 points. Merely rolling over the back is insufficient. Special circumstances can add points, including a five-point award, so the guide marks these as ordinary values. |
| A pin ends the match despite the running score | NFHS Rule 5-11-1, printed page 24: both shoulders or scapula in continuous mat contact for two seconds. The referee determines the fall. |
| Standing up alone is not an escape | NFHS control and escape definitions, Rules 5-6 and 5-10. Control can persist while standing. |
| Three periods with later position choices | NFHS Rules 6-2 and 6-3, printed pages 33–34. Youth timing and overtime must be confirmed for the event. |
| Technical fall uses a 15-point advantage | NFHS Rule 9-1-7 and the 2025–26 clarification. If earned during qualifying near-fall action, that action can continue. Continuation ends when near-fall criteria cease; this is not necessarily the end of the entire pinning situation. |
| Worked example finishes Green 5, Red 4 | Original teaching example applying the verified rules: Green takedown 3–0; Red escape 3–1; Red takedown 3–4; Green reversal 5–4. These are cumulative individual scores. |
| A pin is not six individual match points | NFHS Rule 9-2, printed page 50. Standard dual-team results contribute 3/4/5/6 team points; this table is an optional backup, not a claim that every youth meet keeps team totals. |
| One loss does not necessarily end the day | Different meet, pool and bracket formats exist. The MMWL member guide describes multiple regular-season bouts and separate postseason structures. No universal elimination rule is taught. |
| Parent support should focus on development | Grindstone's stated philosophy and USA Wrestling's parent and athlete-development resources support encouragement, patience and learning beyond early results. The suggested sentence “I loved watching you wrestle” is an original example, not a claimed source quotation. |
| Report suspicious skin conditions and maintain hygiene | Current CDC athlete guidance supports reporting, prompt assessment, showering, laundering used clothing and avoiding shared towels. Covering a suspected communicable condition is not permission to wrestle. |
| Suspected concussion means stopping participation | CDC HEADS UP: remove immediately, no same-day return, and healthcare evaluation and clearance for return. The materials do not provide a diagnosis or an accelerated return protocol. |
| Growing athletes should eat and hydrate normally | USA Wrestling youth guidance and the American Academy of Pediatrics support rejecting rapid weight loss and dehydration. The guide gives no weight-loss targets. |

## Event details for the presenter to confirm

Use the current coach or organizer message to fill these in before the orientation or first meet. These are variable logistics, not gaps in the basic score lesson.

- Any MMWL scoring or developmental modifications, period lengths and overtime.
- Current uniform, headgear and mouthguard rules, particularly with braces.
- Arrival/check-in time, the location and timing of weigh-ins, and how weight changes are reported.
- Required athlete membership, event registration and spectator admission.
- Team meeting place, how bout/mat changes are announced, and coach coverage on multiple mats.
- Departure procedure, tournament format, event medical contact and how families raise a pairing concern.

Do not carry another club's app, departure rules, travel estimates or eligibility advice into Grindstone policy. Do not carry USA Wrestling national-event exceptions into MMWL by assumption. For example, the current USA Wrestling folkstyle addendum and the published 2026 MSWA Future Champions Finale instructions differ on headgear, mouthguards and coach presence. That is why the parent materials tell families to bring the equipment and confirm the actual event requirements.

## Parent and spectator code of conduct — added September 25, 2026

The accompanying one-page code adds the organizer-requested expectation that parents and guests remain in the spectator area unless their child is wrestling or on deck (next to wrestle). Even then, the host must permit spectator access. Families use designated viewing space, keep the wrestling surface, coaching areas, scoring table and walkways clear, and return to the spectator area afterward. This is program orientation guidance supplied for this packet, not a claim about a published MMWL or USA Wrestling access rule. The handout, slide 10 and speaker script carry the same guidance.

The code should be read alongside [Carroll County’s Good Sportsmanship Policy/Code of Conduct](https://www.carrollcountymd.gov/media/17339/good_sportsmanship_policy_interactive_2023.pdf), which applies to Recreation Council programs, parents and guests. It supports positive encouragement, respect for participants and officials, leaving coaching to coaches, and arranging a time and place to discuss disagreements with coaches. The new one-page code links to the county policy; it does not replace that policy or invent a disciplinary sequence. Coaches handle scoring and rules questions with officials. Urgent safety concerns should be brought to event staff immediately.

[USA Wrestling’s “Coaching From the Stands”](https://www.themat.com/news/2018/march/01/coaching-from-the-stands), March 1, 2018, supports positive fan behavior and giving the athlete room to hear the coach. This article is parent advice, not a binding event-access rule. Both sources were checked for the conduct addition; the underlying match-scoring research is unchanged.

## Common penalty explanations — added September 26, 2026

The added page explains six useful categories for new parents: starting infractions, stalling, locked hands, grabbing gear and other technical violations, illegal holds, and unnecessary roughness or wrestler unsportsmanlike conduct during a match. These are selected for parent usefulness, not ranked by measured frequency. A standalone Word/PDF copy is also available. The main handout keeps the FAQ on page 3 and adds penalties on page 4. Optional slide 16 and its notes provide details; the main speech includes a brief explanation and remains approximately ten minutes.

The [NFHS 2024–25 rulebook hosted by Utah USA Wrestling](https://www.utahwrestling.org/NFHSWrestlingRuleBook24_25.pdf), Rules 5, 7 and 8 and the penalty charts at printed pages 44–45, distinguishes three sequences within a match. False starts and incorrect starting positions share two cautions, then one opponent point for each later infraction. Stalling is counted separately: warning, 1 point, 1 point, 2 points, disqualification. Illegal holds, technical violations, unnecessary roughness and wrestler unsportsmanlike conduct during the match share a cumulative 1-point, 1-point, 2-point, disqualification sequence without an initial warning. Flagrant misconduct has separate immediate disqualification provisions. The guide does not apply the wrestler match-point sequence to coach or spectator misconduct.

These details were checked against the [current NFHS rules changes](https://www.nfhs.org/sports/wrestling/rules) and [2026–27 comments](https://www.nfhs.org/resources/sports/wrestling-comments-on-the-rules-2026-27). The 2026–27 boundary clarification distinguishes neutral-position stalling at the edge from ordinary wrestling action. A normal boundary stop, stalemate or precautionary safety stoppage does not itself establish a penalty. The guide preserves standing, lifting and near-fall exceptions for locked hands; it does not teach that all hand clasps are illegal.

[USA Wrestling section 25](https://www.usawmembership.com/usa_wrestling_rule_book) adopts NFHS scoring with USAW event modifications and explicitly emphasizes intervention in potentially dangerous positions, especially for 14U and younger athletes. USAW national-event modifications are not assumed to be MMWL policy. Confirm youth or host modifications with the coach. All penalty-point sequences described add points to the opponent; they do not subtract points from the offending wrestler’s score.
